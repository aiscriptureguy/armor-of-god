# Project Brief — Armor of God

**Status:** Gate 1 approved — Phase 2 active

---

## Study topic

The "armor of God" metaphor as presented across the Bible (primarily Ephesians 6:10–18), the Book of Mormon, and the Doctrine & Covenants. The study examines each named piece of armor, its spiritual meaning, its scriptural context, and its application to modern discipleship.

## Study boundaries (what is explicitly out of scope)

- General Christian military/warfare metaphors not specifically tied to the armor of God passages
- Comparative religion or non-Latter-day Saint theological interpretations (except where an ESV translation difference is flagged for review)
- Church history or institutional history not directly illuminating the armor passages
- Deep biography of Paul or his broader letter to the Ephesians beyond what illuminates the armor passage

## Intended audience

Personal study — the primary user is the project owner, Chase. The site should also be usable by any adult member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints who wants a thoughtful, visually engaging reference on this topic.

## Central questions the site should help answer

1. How do you put on the whole armor of God in today's world?
2. Why were these specific pieces of armor chosen? What bodily vital parts do they protect, and what is the spiritual parallel?
3. The sword is the only explicitly offensive piece — what does that asymmetry mean spiritually?
4. How does the armor of God metaphor appear across the Bible, Book of Mormon, and D&C, and do the different volumes add distinct meaning?
5. What does each piece of armor look like practiced in modern daily life?

## What the user already believes (pre-research settled assumptions)

- The sword (word of God) is the only offensive piece; all others are defensive.
- There is likely intentional reasoning behind which body parts each piece protects and what that suggests spiritually.
- The metaphor has rich cross-scriptural depth worth documenting.

## What the user wants to discover rather than assume

- Whether the specific body-protection logic is consistent and meaningful or incidental.
- All canonical passages across all four standard works that touch the armor of God theme.
- Historical and cultural context: what did a Roman soldier's actual armor look like, and how does that inform Paul's metaphor?
- Greek and Hebrew word meanings behind each armor piece.
- How official Church sources (General Conference, manuals, Gospel Topics) teach and apply each piece.
- Whether ESV or other translation choices for the biblical passages reveal anything the KJV wording obscures (flagged for review, not automatically published).
- Answers to as many specific metaphor questions as the research can support.

## Desired depth

Full breakdown per armor piece:
- Greek/Hebrew word study
- Historical context (Roman military equipment)
- KJV text with ESV comparison flagged for translation differences
- Cross-references across Bible, Book of Mormon, and D&C
- Official Church teaching (General Conference, manuals)
- Spiritual meaning: what body part it protects and why that matters
- Modern application: what putting on this piece looks like today
- Q&A format for lingering interpretive questions

## Site type

Hybrid — a single-page interactive visual reference with guided depth. A visitor can explore the armor diagram interactively and also read structured study content below.

## Expected passage count and types

- 1 primary passage (Ephesians 6:10–18) examined in full detail
- Several secondary passages from Isaiah, 1 Thessalonians, and possibly 2 Nephi, Mosiah, and D&C 27 — exact list to be determined during research
- Supporting verses from General Conference talks and official Church study helps
- Approximate total: 10–25 distinct scripture references depending on what research surfaces

## Church scripture text and ESV relationship

Church sources (KJV and Church scripture) are the default and primary text displayed on the site. ESV wording will be compared during research only. If any ESV translation differs meaningfully from the KJV in a way that changes interpretation, it will be flagged for Chase's review before any ESV text appears on the site. No ESV text will be published without explicit approval and the required copyright notice.

## Accessibility and mobile priorities

- Desktop-primary experience; mobile-friendly is required but secondary
- No specific accessibility needs beyond standard good practice (color contrast, keyboard access, readable type)
- Touch-tap interactions required for the armor diagram on mobile (no hover-only interactions)

## Visual style preferences

- **Anchor image:** A cartoonish warrior figure in a watercolor painting style — the interactive diagram
- **Palette:** Light and airy (not dark or dramatic)
- **Feel:** Decorative and classical — illuminated-manuscript warmth, not minimal tech
- **Typography:** Classical serif feel for scripture text; clean readability overall
- **Interaction:** Click/tap each armor piece on the warrior figure to reveal details
- **Layout below the diagram:** Q&A sections, interesting tidbits, scripture cross-reference chart
- **Disliked:** Dark backgrounds, sensationalism, excessive gamification
