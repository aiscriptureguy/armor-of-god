# Doctrine and Covenants 27:15–18

- Passage ID: PASS-0007
- Testament or volume: Doctrine and Covenants
- Speaker or narrative voice: Jesus Christ speaking in first person directly to Joseph Smith
- Audience: Joseph Smith and the early Restoration Church
- Immediate setting: August 1830, Harmony, Pennsylvania. Joseph was going to purchase wine for a sacrament meeting, was met by a heavenly messenger, and received this revelation. The armor passage (vv. 15–18) follows immediately after a vision of all the priesthood holders from every dispensation who will participate in a future millennial sacrament (vv. 5–14).
- Broader scriptural setting: The Restoration — the Church is four months old. The dispensation of the fulness of times, the gathering of all things in one (v. 13).
- Church scripture URL: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/27?lang=eng
- Approved comparison translation: n/a (Restoration scripture)

## The critical context: vv. 1–14

D&C 27 opens with sacrament instructions (vv. 1–4), then the Lord names every dispensation's key figure who will participate in a future millennial sacrament:

- **Moroni** (v. 5) — keys of the Book of Mormon, fulness of the everlasting gospel
- **Elias** (v. 6) — keys of the restoration of all things
- **John the Baptist** (vv. 7–8) — who ordained Joseph and Oliver to the Aaronic Priesthood
- **Elijah** (v. 9) — keys of turning hearts of fathers to children
- **Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph** (v. 10) — the ancient patriarchs
- **Michael / Adam** (v. 11) — father of all, prince of all, ancient of days
- **Peter, James, and John** (v. 12) — who ordained Joseph and Oliver as apostles
- **All those the Father has given** (v. 14)

Verse 15 begins immediately: *"Wherefore, lift up your hearts and rejoice... and take upon you my whole armor."*

**Observation:** The armor command is given in the context of the fulness of times — all priesthood keys restored, all dispensations gathered, the entire family of God assembled. This is not a general self-help instruction; it is a command issued at the opening of the last dispensation, by the Lord himself, to the prophet and the Church.

## Textual observations

- **The speaker is Jesus Christ** — first person throughout. "My whole armor," "my Spirit," "my word which I reveal unto you." This is the Lord personally issuing this command, not Paul writing to a congregation.
- **"My whole armor"** (v. 15) — the possessive "my" is Christ's own armor. Compare Ephesians 6:11 "the whole armour of God" (third person). D&C makes the ownership explicit.
- **"Which I have sent mine angels to commit unto you"** (v. 16) — the gospel of peace is not a general concept; in the D&C it is specifically the restored gospel brought by angels. This connects the shoes directly to the Restoration: Moroni, John the Baptist, Elias, Elijah, Peter, James and John (all named in vv. 5–12) are the angels who committed the gospel.
- **"Sword of my Spirit, which I will pour out upon you"** (v. 18) — the sword is not merely picked up; it is promised to be poured out by the Lord. This is a covenant, not just an instruction.
- **"My word which I reveal unto you"** (v. 18) — a second sword element added in the D&C. Alongside the Spirit, ongoing prophetic revelation is named as a spiritual weapon. This extends the sword from past scripture to living, present-tense revelation.
- **"Be agreed as touching all things whatsoever ye ask of me"** (v. 18) — unity in prayer appended. No counterpart in Ephesians. The armor is worn in covenant community, not just individually.
- **"Be faithful until I come"** (v. 18) — the entire armor passage closes with the Second Coming. The armor is endurance gear — wear it until the Lord returns.

## What the Restoration context adds

*(Interpretive — labeled as such)*

The D&C version is not merely a restatement of Ephesians 6. It is the same armor, now spoken by the Lord himself, in the context of the restored Church at the opening of the last dispensation. Several meanings are deepened:

1. **The armor is Christ's own** — wearing it is putting on his character and gifts, not just following a checklist
2. **The gospel of peace is the restored gospel** — the shoes have a specific Restoration referent: the angels of the Restoration are the ones who committed this gospel
3. **The sword includes living revelation** — "my word which I reveal unto you" extends the sword beyond canonized scripture to ongoing prophetic direction
4. **The Spirit will be poured out as a covenant** — this is not passive; the Lord promises active bestowal
5. **The armor is eschatological** — it is worn unto the Second Coming and connects to the covenant promise of being gathered to where Christ is

## D&C 35:13–15 — related but distinct

D&C 35:13–14 uses overlapping martial language: "I will be their shield and their buckler... I will gird up their loins... I will let fall the sword in their behalf." Here God himself is the armor — he is the shield and buckler acting on behalf of the saints. This is the Isaiah 59 pattern (God as warrior-protector), not the D&C 27 / Ephesians 6 pattern (saints putting on God's armor). These are complementary pictures: sometimes God wears the armor for us; sometimes he instructs us to wear it ourselves.
