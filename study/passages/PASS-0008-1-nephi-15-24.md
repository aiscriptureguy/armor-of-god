## Full text (verified 2026-10-26)

> 24 And I said unto them that it was the word of God; and whoso would hearken unto the word of God, and would hold fast unto it, they would never perish; neither could the temptations and the fiery darts of the adversary overpower them unto blindness, to lead them away to destruction.

---

## Textual observations

- The iron rod = the word of God (explicit equation)
- The protection requires two active conditions: "hearken unto" AND "hold fast unto" — not passive, not one-time
- The named threat: "the fiery darts of the adversary" — same phrase used in Ephesians 6:16 (KJV: "fiery darts of the wicked") and D&C 27:17
- The fiery darts lead to "blindness" — a specific consequence not named in Ephesians or D&C. The attack targets spiritual sight.
- The final destination of the unprotected: "destruction"
- Cross-referenced directly from D&C 27:17 footnote ("fiery") and Ephesians 6:16 footnote ("darts")

---

## What this passage adds to the armor study

- In Ephesians 6, the **shield of faith** quenches the fiery darts. In 1 Nephi 15:24, the **word of God** (iron rod) protects against the fiery darts. Both the shield and the word protect against the same attack — the Book of Mormon merges these two pieces.
- **"Hold fast unto it"** — the iron rod must be actively grasped and maintained. This adds a kinetic, ongoing dimension to the word-of-God-as-weapon that Ephesians' sword image suggests but doesn't state as explicitly.
- **Blindness as the specific danger** — the adversary's darts don't just defeat; they obscure. Spiritual blindness is the mechanism: darts cloud vision, which leads to wandering away, which leads to destruction. This explains *how* the attacks work, not just that they're dangerous.
