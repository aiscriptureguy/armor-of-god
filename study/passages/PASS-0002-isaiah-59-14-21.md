# Isaiah 59:14–21

- Passage ID: PASS-0002
- Testament or volume: Old Testament
- Speaker or narrative voice: Isaiah (prophetic voice on behalf of the Lord)
- Audience: Israel / Judah; also Messianic in scope
- Immediate setting: Israel has confessed moral failure — truth fallen in the street, justice absent, no intercessor (vv. 9–15). God responds by acting himself.
- Broader scriptural setting: Isaiah's prophecy of redemption and the coming of the Redeemer to Zion (vv. 20–21)
- Church scripture URL: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/59?lang=eng
- Approved comparison translation: ESV (research only — not approved for publication)
- Source IDs: SRC-0001, SRC-0007

## Full KJV text (verses 14–21, verified 2026-10-26)

> 14 And judgment is turned away backward, and justice standeth afar off: for truth is fallen in the street, and equity cannot enter.
>
> 15 Yea, truth faileth; and he that departeth from evil maketh himself a prey: and the Lord saw it, and it displeased him that there was no judgment.
>
> 16 And he saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor: therefore his arm brought salvation unto him; and his righteousness, it sustained him.
>
> 17 For he put on righteousness as a breastplate, and an helmet of salvation upon his head; and he put on the garments of vengeance for clothing, and was clad with zeal as a cloak.
>
> 18 According to their deeds, accordingly he will repay, fury to his adversaries, recompence to his enemies; to the islands he will repay recompence.
>
> 19 So shall they fear the name of the Lord from the west, and his glory from the rising of the sun. When the enemy shall come in like a flood, the Spirit of the Lord shall lift up a standard against him.
>
> 20 And the Redeemer shall come to Zion, and unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob, saith the Lord.
>
> 21 As for me, this is my covenant with them, saith the Lord; My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed, saith the Lord, from henceforth and for ever.

## Armor pieces named (v. 17)

| Piece | KJV phrase | Spiritual quality | Present in Ephesians 6? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breastplate | "righteousness as a breastplate" | Righteousness | Yes — same piece, same quality |
| Helmet | "an helmet of salvation upon his head" | Salvation | Yes — same piece, same quality |
| Garments | "garments of vengeance for clothing" | Vengeance | No — Paul omits this entirely |
| Cloak | "clad with zeal as a cloak" | Zeal | No — Paul omits this entirely |

**Observation:** Paul preserves the breastplate of righteousness and helmet of salvation verbatim from Isaiah 59:17. He drops the garments of vengeance and cloak of zeal entirely — removing the retributive elements — and adds the belt, shoes, shield, and sword.

---

## Textual observations

- **The wearer is God, not a believer.** Isaiah 59 describes the Lord himself putting on armor because no human could — "he saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor" (v. 16).
- **The trigger for God arming himself** is the moral collapse of Israel: truth is fallen in the street (v. 14), no intercessor exists (v. 16). God acts because humanity failed.
- **The armor leads directly to redemption.** The same passage ends with "the Redeemer shall come to Zion" (v. 20) and a covenant about the Spirit and God's words not departing (v. 21).
- **The KJV footnote for Ephesians 6:14** (breastplate, footnote 14c) cross-references Isaiah 59:17 and 61:10 — confirming the editorial connection.
- **The footnote for Isaiah 59:17** cross-references Isaiah 11:5, 61:10, Romans 13:12, and Ephesians 6:14 — a confirmed cross-reference chain across Old and New Testaments.

## Connections to other passages

- Isaiah 11:5 (PASS-0003): Christ/the Branch also wears a belt of righteousness — the Messianic connection
- Isaiah 61:10: "he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness" — third Isaiah armor passage
- Ephesians 6:14–17 (PASS-0001): Paul draws directly from this passage; keeps breastplate and helmet; removes vengeance/zeal; adds belt, shoes, shield, sword
- Romans 13:12 (PASS-0005): "put on the armour of light" and "put on the Lord Jesus Christ"

---

## Possible patterns

- **The wearer shift:** In Isaiah 59, God wears the armor. In Ephesians 6, believers wear the same armor. This may be Paul's way of saying the armor God used to redeem Israel is now given to his people. (See PAT-0002)
- **Paul's list omits vengeance:** The garments of vengeance and cloak of zeal that describe God's judgment are absent from Paul's list entirely. Paul's version of the armor has no retributive element — it is purely protective and redemptive. *(That this omission is intentional on Paul's part is an interpretive reading; the texts do not state his reasoning.)*

---

## Interpretive cautions

- Whether Paul consciously drew from Isaiah 59:17 or arrived at the same imagery independently cannot be known with certainty; the verbal and conceptual overlap is strong but Paul does not quote Isaiah explicitly here
- The Isaiah passage describes divine judgment; caution in reading the Ephesians armor as equally retributive — Paul's version is not
