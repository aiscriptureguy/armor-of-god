## Full text (verified 2026-10-26)

**Alma 56:47–48:**
> 47 Now they never had fought, yet they did not fear death; and they did think more upon the liberty of their fathers than they did upon their lives; yea, they had been taught by their mothers, that if they did not doubt, God would deliver them.
>
> 48 And they rehearsed unto me the words of their mothers, saying: We do not doubt our mothers knew it.

**Alma 57:25–27:**
> 25 And it came to pass that there were two hundred, out of my two thousand and sixty, who had fainted because of the loss of blood; nevertheless, according to the goodness of God, and to our great astonishment, and also the joy of our whole army, there was not one soul of them who did perish; yea, and neither was there one soul among them who had not received many wounds.
>
> 26 And now, their preservation was astonishing to our whole army, yea, that they should be spared while there was a thousand of our brethren who were slain. And we do justly ascribe it to the miraculous power of God, because of their exceeding faith in that which they had been taught to believe—that there was a just God, and whosoever did not doubt, that they should be preserved by his marvelous power.
>
> 27 Now this was the faith of these of whom I have spoken; they are young, and their minds are firm, and they do put their trust in God continually.

## Textual observations

- 2,060 young men entered battle; 2,060 survived — not one died, though every one was wounded
- The text explicitly attributes their survival to "the miraculous power of God, because of their exceeding faith" (v. 26)
- The specific content of their faith: "that there was a just God, and whosoever did not doubt, that they should be preserved by his marvelous power"
- The source of that faith: their mothers — "they had been taught by their mothers" (56:47)
- The young men's confirmation: "We do not doubt our mothers knew it" (56:48) — testimony built on inherited, verified trust
- "Their minds are firm" (57:27) — the helmet of salvation protecting the head/mind is the closest armor parallel
- "Put their trust in God continually" (57:27) — ongoing, sustained faith, not a single act

---

## What this passage adds to the armor study

- **Faith as demonstrated protection** — this is not a metaphor. The stripling warriors' faith functioned as literal armor. 2,060 wounded, 0 killed. Their faith shielded them.
- **The shield of faith in action** — Ephesians 6:16 says the shield of faith "quenches the fiery darts." The stripling warriors show what that looks like in lived experience: complete preservation despite complete exposure to danger.
- **"Do not doubt"** — the specific mechanism of the shield is not doubting. Faith that wavers does not fully deploy. This is the clearest Book of Mormon definition of how faith as armor actually works.
- **Mothers as the source of armoring** — the faith that protected these men was first given to them by their mothers. Parents teaching faith to their children is the act of putting the armor on the next generation.
- **"Minds are firm"** — connects specifically to the helmet of salvation (head / thoughts / intellect). Firmness of mind as a protective quality.

## Note on physical armor (Alma 43:18–21)

Earlier in the war chapters, Captain Moroni prepares the Nephites with physical armor — breastplates, arm-shields, head-plates — while the Lamanites are essentially unarmored. The Lamanites are "exceedingly afraid... because of their armor, notwithstanding their number being so much greater than the Nephites" (Alma 43:21, SRC-0009). The stripling warriors demonstrate the spiritual counterpart: physical preparation alone does not explain their survival; faith is explicitly named as the cause.

The Book of Mormon thus holds both: physical preparation matters (Alma 43), AND faith provides a protection that physical armor alone cannot (Alma 56–57).
