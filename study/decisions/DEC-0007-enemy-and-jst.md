## Decision 1: JS-H 1:15–17 and enemy identity — site treatment

**Question:** Should the enemy context (JS-H 1:15–17, Ephesians 6:12 hierarchy) appear on the site, and how?

**Decision:** Yes — as a clickable "faded darkness" element surrounding the warrior figure on the interactive diagram. The darkness is part of the visual composition; clicking it opens an info panel about the enemy. This sets the context for why the armor is needed before the visitor explores each piece.

**Visual spec:** Faded darkness surrounding the warrior (not a solid barrier — atmospheric, ominous). Clickable. Consistent with the light/airy watercolor palette but conveys the adversarial dimension.

**Content for this panel:** To be decided — see Decision 2 below.

## Decision 2: Greek hierarchy vs. simple framing for the enemy panel

**Question:** Present the four-title Greek hierarchy (archai, exousiai, kosmokratores, pneumatika) or keep it simple as "Satan and his hosts / organized spiritual opposition"?

**Decision:** Option B — layered. The darkness panel includes:
1. Opening: "we wrestle not against flesh and blood" (Eph. 6:12)
2. The four titles with one-line explanations (principalities, powers, rulers of darkness, spiritual wickedness in high places)
3. The JS-H 1:15–17 connection — Joseph Smith's Sacred Grove encounter as a real-world example of these powers in action
